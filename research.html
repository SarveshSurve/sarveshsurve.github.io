Research

It was not intentional but I have only worked anaerobic microbes, starting with Lactobacilli, followed by Bacteroides and Gracilibacteria.

Fun Fact: Genus name of every bug I have worked on changed before I could publish my first paper in the field.

Bacteroides as a probiotic for the CF gut

The gut microbiome of people with CF differs from that of people without it and lacks Bacteroides. I'm testing whether Bacteroides strains can be evolved to grow better under conditions that mimic the CF gut. I sequence the evolved strains to find the mutations and genome rearrangements behind the improvement, including diversity-generating retroelements (DGRs) and invertible promoters, and ask which of them help the bacteria tolerate bile acids.

The goal is a probiotic strain suited to the CF gut. This work is covered by a pending patent application (WO2026/169644), on which I'm a named inventor, and is now the basis of a startup. In 2024 I did customer discovery for it through NSF I-Corps (Interior Northeast).

Related papers: an in vitro medium for CF gut dysbiosis, Bacteroides and the host response to lung infection, bile acids in CF stool.

Culturing the CF gut

To study gut bacteria you first have to grow them. I culture bacteria from colonoscopy samples from people with and without CF, compare what grows in different regions of the colon, and sequence the isolates. This produced a regional map of the colon culturome and the 109 genomes described in our genome announcement. I also manage the lab's repository of CF samples (~3000) and clinical isolates (~1200).

One isolate came from the esophagus of an adult carrying one CF allele and turned out to be a new species, Streptococcus ruoffii. The description is in review, with Axel Abrahamson, an undergraduate I mentor, as first author.

Papers: the colon culturome, 109 gut bacterial genomes, S. ruoffii sp. nov.

The CF Kids Cohort and CFTR modulators

The CF Kids Cohort is a 17-year longitudinal collection for gut microbiome research. I'm analyzing its gut metagenomes from people with CF before, during, and after CFTR modulator therapy, to see how the gut microbiome changes with treatment. I'm also writing a review of the cohort as a resource for other groups.

Manuscripts in preparation: gut metagenomes and modulator therapy, the cohort review.

Gracilibacteria and microbial dark matter

In summer 2025, in the Microbial Diversity course at the Marine Biological Laboratory, I worked on Gracilibacteria, a group of bacteria that is part of microbial dark matter. In green sulfur bacteria enrichment cultures from a tidal marsh, I used imaging and metagenomics to see where Gracilibacteria sit relative to the green sulfur bacteria and what their genomes encode. To our knowledge this was the first time the group has been grown in culture.

The poster won the Outstanding Presentation award at the 2026 Microbial Dark Matter Symposium. A manuscript is in preparation.

Manuscript: spatial and genomic ecology of Gracilibacteria.

Probiotic lactic acid bacteria (2018–2021)

Before Dartmouth, in Ram Kulkarni's lab at Symbiosis in Pune, I isolated Lactiplantibacillus plantarum strains from Indian foods, then sequenced and compared their genomes. I used adaptive laboratory evolution to produce strains that tolerate furfural and 5-HMF, inhibitors found in the plant-biomass hydrolysates used to make lactic acid. Follow-up work identified a furfural reductase and measured what laboratory domestication does to these strains.

I also cloned and characterized biosynthetic enzymes from Aquilaria malaccensis (agarwood) in E. coli and yeast.

Papers: Scientific Reports 2022, domestication, furfural reductase, adaptive evolution.

Methods

Most of my analysis is in R and Python on Linux: genome assembly and annotation, variant analysis, comparative genomics, phylogenetics, transcriptomics, and metagenomics. At the bench, I do anaerobic culturing, molecular cloning, bacterial genetics, and experimental evolution.
